What is the P-Shot?

The P-Shot, formally known as the Priapus Shot, is a revolutionary regenerative medicine treatment developed by Dr. Charles Runels. It harnesses the body's own healing power through platelet-rich plasma (PRP) to treat erectile dysfunction at its source.

Unlike pharmaceutical treatments that merely mask symptoms, the P-Shot stimulates actual tissue regeneration within the penile shaft and glans. The procedure uses growth factors extracted directly from your own blood, eliminating the risk of allergic reactions or rejection.

Key Innovation: The P-Shot represents a paradigm shift from symptom management to tissue regeneration. By activating your body's natural healing cascade, the treatment creates lasting improvements in erectile function.

How PRP Works at the Cellular Level

Platelet-rich plasma contains concentrated growth factors including:

  • VEGF (Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor): Stimulates new blood vessel formation
  • PDGF (Platelet-Derived Growth Factor): Promotes cellular proliferation and tissue repair
  • TGF-β (Transforming Growth Factor Beta): Regulates cellular differentiation and tissue remodeling
  • FGF (Fibroblast Growth Factor): Enhances collagen production and tissue elasticity

How the P-Shot Works: Step-by-Step Process

The P-Shot procedure is a carefully choreographed process that transforms your blood into regenerative medicine in just 45 minutes.

1

Blood Draw

Your physician draws 30-50ml of venous blood into sterile collection tubes containing sodium citrate anticoagulant. This is equivalent to a standard lab blood test. No special preparation is needed.

Duration: 5 minutes

2

Centrifugation

The blood is placed in a specialized centrifuge machine that spins at 3000 RPM for 15 minutes. This separates the blood into three layers: red blood cells (bottom), platelet-rich plasma (middle), and platelet-poor plasma (top). The middle layer is carefully extracted.

Duration: 15 minutes | Concentration: 2-8x platelet concentration

3

Topical Anaesthetic Application

While PRP is being processed, a topical anaesthetic cream is applied to the penile tissue. The numbing agent penetrates the skin, creating a comfortable sensation. Most patients report this as the most tedious part of the procedure.

Duration: 20-30 minutes | Agents used: Lidocaine or EMLA cream

4

Precise PRP Injection

Using ultrasound or direct visualization guidance, your physician injects the concentrated PRP into specific anatomical sites: the corpus cavernosum (main erectile tissue) and the glans (head of the penis). Precise placement is critical for optimal tissue regeneration. Multiple injection sites ensure even distribution of growth factors.

Duration: 10 minutes | Injection sites: 5-7 strategic locations

5

Recovery and Observation

You'll be observed for 30 minutes post-procedure. Most men report minimal discomfort and can return to light activities immediately. The injection sites may feel slightly tender but this resolves within 24-48 hours. Avoid strenuous activity and sexual intercourse for 48 hours.

Duration: 30 minutes observation | Return to normal: 48-72 hours

6

Biological Response

Over the following weeks and months, the growth factors trigger a cascade of biological healing: angiogenesis (new blood vessel formation), neuronal repair, cellular proliferation, and tissue remodeling. This gradual transformation creates lasting improvements in erectile function.

Timeline: 2 weeks to 6 months

Frequently Asked Questions About the P-Shot

Is the P-Shot painful?

The procedure involves minimal discomfort. Topical anaesthetic is applied 20-30 minutes before injection, and most patients report only mild tingling or pressure sensation. Local anaesthesia options are available for sensitive patients. Pain is typically rated 1-2/10 on a pain scale.

How many P-Shot sessions do I need?

Most patients require a single P-Shot session for initial treatment. Some may benefit from a second session 4-6 weeks later for enhanced results, particularly in cases of severe ED. Most clinicians recommend one procedure initially, with repeat treatment after 12-18 months if needed.

How long does the P-Shot last?

Results typically last 12-18 months. Studies show sustained improvements at 18-month follow-up in 60-70% of patients. Maintenance sessions are optional and can extend benefits further. Some patients maintain benefits beyond 18 months.

Can I take Viagra with the P-Shot?

Yes. Many patients continue using Viagra or Cialis during P-Shot recovery. Combining treatments can provide additional benefits while the PRP regenerates tissue. Discuss with your physician about dosage adjustments as function improves.

Is the P-Shot safe?

The P-Shot has been safely performed on thousands of patients worldwide. Using your own blood components eliminates the risk of allergic reactions or rejection. Serious complications are rare (<1%) when performed by qualified urologists in accredited facilities.

How is P-Shot different from dermal fillers?

Unlike injectable fillers (hyaluronic acid, PMMA), the P-Shot regenerates penile tissue using growth factors from your own blood. It addresses the underlying cause of ED rather than providing temporary volume. P-Shot can increase both erectile function and modest girth; fillers only add volume.

When will I see results from the P-Shot?

Weeks 2-4: Initial improvements in blood flow and sensitivity. Months 2-3: Peak effects as angiogenesis completes and tissue regenerates. Months 6-18: Results continue to improve as new tissue matures and vascular changes stabilize.

What if the P-Shot doesn't work for me?

Clinical studies show 70-80% of patients experience significant improvement. If you don't respond within 3 months, alternative treatments like shockwave therapy or low-dose tadalafil can be explored. Some non-responders benefit from a second P-Shot session.

Can I have sex after the P-Shot?

It's recommended to wait 24-48 hours before sexual activity to allow healing and prevent infection. You can resume normal activities immediately, though the injection site may be slightly tender. Most patients can have sex within 48 hours without risk.
