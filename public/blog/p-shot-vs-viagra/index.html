P-Shot vs Viagra: Which ED Treatment is Right for You?

By Dr. Robert Chen, Andrologist — Medically Reviewed

Erectile dysfunction affects over 50% of men over 40, and the treatment landscape has never been more varied. For decades, Viagra (sildenafil) was the default solution. Today, regenerative treatments like the P-Shot offer a fundamentally different approach. So which is right for you?

How Each Treatment Works

Viagra (sildenafil) works by inhibiting the PDE5 enzyme, which causes smooth muscle relaxation and increased blood flow to the penis when sexually stimulated. It is a symptomatic treatment — it doesn't fix the underlying problem; it temporarily works around it. Viagra must be taken 30–60 minutes before sexual activity and requires sexual stimulation to work.

The P-Shot uses platelet-rich plasma (PRP) derived from your own blood. The PRP is injected into penile tissue, releasing growth factors (PDGF, VEGF, TGF-β) that stimulate new blood vessel formation (angiogenesis), nerve regeneration and improved smooth muscle function. Results are spontaneous and do not require planning or pill-taking.

Who Should Choose Viagra?

  • Men with mild, occasional ED who are satisfied with on-demand treatment
  • Men whose ED is primarily psychological or situational
  • Those who prefer a non-invasive, low-commitment option
  • Men without cardiovascular conditions that contraindicate PDE5 inhibitors

Who Should Choose the P-Shot?

  • Men with moderate to severe vascular ED who find oral medications insufficient
  • Men who want spontaneous erections without planning or pill-taking
  • Those who have tried Viagra and experienced inadequate results or side effects
  • Men seeking a long-term, root-cause solution rather than ongoing medication
  • Those with Peyronie's disease (P-Shot has shown benefit for penile curvature)

Can You Combine Them?

Yes. Some men use Viagra in the weeks immediately following their P-Shot while the PRP takes effect (4–8 weeks), then discontinue oral medication as spontaneous erections return. Combining P-Shot with shockwave therapy is more clinically established and produces superior long-term results.
