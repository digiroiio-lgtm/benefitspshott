P-Shot vs Viagra: Which ED Treatment is Right for You?

By Dr. Robert Chen, Andrologist

The P-Shot and Viagra work through fundamentally different mechanisms: Viagra (sildenafil) temporarily increases blood flow by inhibiting PDE5 enzymes, while the P-Shot uses platelet-rich plasma to permanently regenerate penile tissue and blood vessels. Viagra requires planning and must be taken 30-60 minutes before sexual activity, whereas P-Shot results are spontaneous once achieved. For men with moderate to severe vascular ED who have found oral medications unsatisfactory, the P-Shot represents a more durable, root-cause solution.

Can Erectile Dysfunction Be Cured? What the Science Says

By Dr. Mehmet Yilmaz, Lead Urologist

Whether erectile dysfunction can be permanently cured depends largely on its underlying cause: psychogenic ED and ED caused by reversible lifestyle factors such as obesity or smoking can often be fully resolved, while vascular or neurological ED typically requires ongoing management. Significant advances in regenerative medicine, particularly the P-Shot and low-intensity shockwave therapy, have demonstrated ability to address the root cause of vascular ED and produce long-lasting remission in many patients. Clinical data suggests that lifestyle modifications combined with regenerative treatments offer the best chance of sustained improvement.

Shockwave Therapy for ED: Real Results & What to Expect

By Dr. Sarah Thompson, Urologist

Low-intensity shockwave therapy (Li-ESWT) has emerged as one of the most evidence-backed non-invasive treatments for erectile dysfunction, with multiple randomised controlled trials demonstrating 60-75% improvements in IIEF erectile function scores. Unlike oral medications, shockwave therapy works by stimulating neovascularisation (new blood vessel formation) in penile tissue, addressing the vascular root cause rather than simply masking symptoms. Most patients begin noticing improvements after the third or fourth session, with peak benefits realised at three months post-treatment.
