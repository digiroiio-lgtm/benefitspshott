Can Erectile Dysfunction Be Cured? What the Science Says

By Dr. Mehmet Yilmaz, Lead Urologist — Medically Reviewed

The answer to whether erectile dysfunction can be permanently cured is nuanced — and depends critically on the underlying cause. For many men, meaningful and lasting remission is achievable. For others, optimal management rather than a cure is the goal.

Types of Erectile Dysfunction and Curability

1. Psychogenic ED — Highly Curable

When ED is primarily psychological in origin (performance anxiety, relationship stress, depression), targeted interventions can produce complete and lasting resolution:

  • Cognitive-behavioural therapy (CBT) resolves psychogenic ED in 60–80% of cases
  • Sex therapy and couples counselling are effective for relationship-related ED
  • Treatment of underlying depression or anxiety often resolves ED simultaneously

2. Lifestyle-Related ED — Often Reversible

ED caused by reversible lifestyle factors can often be resolved by addressing the root cause:

  • Obesity: A 10% reduction in body weight improves erectile function in 30% of obese men
  • Smoking: Smoking cessation restores erectile function within 1–2 years in many men
  • Sedentary lifestyle: 30 minutes of aerobic exercise 3× per week improves IIEF scores significantly
  • Alcohol excess: Reducing alcohol to recommended limits often produces meaningful improvement

3. Vascular ED — Advanced Treatments Offer Long-Term Remission

Vascular ED (caused by impaired blood flow) is the most common type in men over 40. While traditional treatment has focused on symptom management (Viagra, injections), regenerative medicine now offers the prospect of addressing the root cause:

  • P-Shot (PRP therapy): Stimulates new blood vessel formation and tissue regeneration; clinical studies show 70–80% improvement in IIEF scores lasting 12–24 months
  • Low-intensity shockwave therapy: Promotes neovascularisation; multiple RCTs demonstrate 60–75% IIEF improvement with results lasting 12+ months
  • Combined P-Shot + shockwave protocol: Produces the most durable results, with some patients achieving sustained erection quality comparable to pre-ED baseline

4. Neurological ED — Partial Improvement Possible

ED caused by nerve damage (post-prostatectomy, diabetes neuropathy, spinal injury) is more challenging. Regenerative treatments can partially restore nerve function and significantly improve quality of erections, though complete cure is less predictable.

5. Hormonal ED — Highly Treatable

When ED is caused by low testosterone or other hormonal imbalances, hormone replacement therapy (HRT) resolves ED in the majority of cases — provided the vascular component is not also significant.

The Role of Lifestyle Modification

Regardless of the primary cause, lifestyle modification is the most evidence-backed intervention for long-term ED improvement. The Massachusetts Male Ageing Study found that men who maintained healthy lifestyles had a 70% lower risk of developing ED compared to sedentary peers. Key interventions:

  • Mediterranean diet (associated with 40% lower ED risk)
  • Regular cardiovascular exercise
  • Healthy weight maintenance
  • Smoking cessation
  • Moderate alcohol consumption
  • Stress management

Our Clinical Recommendation

For men with vascular ED who have not achieved satisfactory results with oral medications, a combined programme of lifestyle modification, low-intensity shockwave therapy (6–12 sessions) and P-Shot offers the best evidence for achieving sustained improvement that goes beyond symptom masking.
